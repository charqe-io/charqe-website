GymA is a mobile application for people who train in the gym. The goal is simple: the athlete should not have to fight with a phone, only focus on lifting. Sets, reps and perceived exertion are recorded in two taps; the rest timer and the move to the next exercise advance on their own.

Key features

  • Automatic flow. When a set ends the rest timer starts by itself, and when it runs out the next set is in front of you. The timer keeps running even if the app is sent to the background.
  • Exercise library. More than 870 exercises with instructions, target muscle groups and equipment alternatives. Custom exercises can be added.
  • Statistics. Seven separate statistics screens covering volume, estimated 1RM, a personal record timeline, muscle group balance and a yearly activity heat map.
  • Body measurements. Nine metrics — weight, body fat, muscle mass and circumference measurements — each with its own trend chart.
  • Offline capable. If the connection drops, the workout stays on the device and synchronises automatically once connectivity returns.

Platforms and status

In development for iOS and Android, with a Turkish and English interface. The application is currently preparing for launch. Smart watch integration is in development.

How the scope grows

Today GymA serves the athlete. The roadmap adds a surface where a coach can follow their own athletes, and a panel where gyms can manage memberships. All three will connect to the same data layer — the record the athlete enters is the same record the coach sees.

Dormz is a family of applications for student dormitories. The site where students find a dorm is live: a student picks their university and compares the dorms around the campus by walking time, price and amenities in a single list, sees them on a map and contacts the dorm directly.

Key features

  • Search by campus. The map puts the campus at its centre and rings show walking time. Dorms around 198 campuses are listed today.
  • Data from official sources. Dorm records are compiled from the Ministry of Youth and Sports private dormitory guide and Council of Higher Education (YÖK) data, then checked against independent sources.
  • Free for students and parents. Searching, comparing and contacting a dorm cost nothing.
  • For dorm owners. Listing is free; dorms that want more visibility can use advertising targeted by campus or city.

How the scope grows

Dormz Manager, the panel where a dorm runs itself, is in development: rooms, students, payments, requests, staff and per-screen permissions. A mobile app for students, parents and staff and a turnstile integration come next. All of them connect to the same data layer.

Wort is a web game that teaches English through play. A hero stands in the middle while monsters carrying Turkish words close in from every side; the moment the player types the English word, the letters become bullets and burst the monster.

How it teaches

  • From scratch. A new word first arrives with its answer shown, then with missing letters, and finally hidden.
  • No progress without knowing. Every stage ends with an exam that asks all of its words; miss a single one and the next stage stays locked.
  • Spaced repetition. A remembered word is asked again the next day, then after 3, 7, 16 and 35 days.
  • Grammar. Each unit has a grammar topic, practised only with words taught up to that unit.

Scope and status

Four levels from A1 to B2: 66 units, 3,504 words and 1,452 grammar sentences. The game map is a path that grows from a village to a town, a city and a metropolis as the level rises. Wort is currently in development.
